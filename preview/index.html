Top 5 Travel Destinations in Bangladesh

📅 Nov 5, 2026 • Travel Guide

Bangladesh is small on the map but huge in variety. If I had to pick only five places to show a first-time traveler, these would be my list — each one a completely different world.

1. Sajek Valley, Rangamati — a sea of clouds below your feet at sunrise. 2. Cox's Bazar — the longest sea beach in the world, best at golden hour. 3. Saint Martin's Island — the country's only coral island, with water so clear you can count the stones.

4. Sylhet's tea gardens and Ratargul — endless green rows and a swamp forest you explore by boat. 5. The Sundarbans — the largest mangrove forest on Earth, silent rivers, and if you're lucky, a glimpse of the Royal Bengal Tiger.

My advice: don't rush. Pick one region per trip, stay at least two nights, and let the place slow you down. That's when Bangladesh really shows itself.

Why I Love Coding

📅 Oct 28, 2026 • Thoughts

Most things in life need money, permission or luck to build. Coding needs only a laptop and an idea. That freedom is what pulled me in — and it's why I keep coming back.

There's a moment every coder knows: the code finally runs, the page loads, the button works. Something that existed only in your head is now real, and anyone in the world can use it. That feeling never gets old.

Coding also teaches patience like nothing else. A missing semicolon can stop everything — and hunting it down teaches you to think clearly, step by step. I believe that mindset helps far beyond the screen.

I'm still learning, and that's the best part. In this field, there's always a next level.

A Peaceful Trip to Sajek Valley

📅 Oct 20, 2026 • Travel Story

The road to Sajek is an adventure by itself — winding hill roads, army checkposts, and views that keep getting better with every turn. But nothing prepares you for the first morning there.

I woke up before sunrise and stepped outside to find a white ocean of clouds below the cottage, with hilltops floating like islands. The whole valley was silent. No horns, no rush — just cold air and slowly changing light.

We walked to Konglak Para, the highest point, and had tea at a tiny shop run by a local family. Simple tea, but at 1,800 feet with that view, it tasted like a celebration.

Sajek reminded me that peace isn't a place you find — it's what happens when the noise finally stops. If you go, stay at least one night. The morning is the whole point.
